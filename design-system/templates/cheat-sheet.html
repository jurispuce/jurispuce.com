Risk Management for Executives · Cheat sheet

The risk lifecycle on one page

Six stages. Each one has a required output. If a stage produces nothing on paper, it didn't happen.

StageRequired outputCommon mistake
01

Draft

One-sentence risk: cause → event → consequence, tied to an objective.

Logging threats ("ransomware") instead of risks.

02

Identified

Named owner with authority over the objective. Linked assets and processes.

Owner = whoever was in the workshop.

03

Analysed

Likelihood and impact with evidence. Existing controls listed and verified.

Scores set from gut feeling, never revisited.

04

Evaluated

Compared against board-approved appetite. Decision: treat, accept, escalate.

Treating everything "red" without an appetite.

05

Treated

New or improved control with owner, budget line and deadline in the action plan.

Treatment = changing the colour on a heatmap.

06

Monitored

KRI with threshold and review date. Movement reported, not static scores.

Closing risks instead of monitoring them.

Four treatment options
  • Mitigate — add or improve a control.
  • Avoid — stop the activity that creates the risk.
  • Transfer — insurance or contract; residual risk stays with you.
  • Accept — a recorded decision, not a default.
Red flags in a register
  • No objective in the risk sentence.
  • Same score for 12+ months.
  • Controls that are documented but never operated.
  • More than 30 risks per owner.
Write a risk in one sentence
Because of [cause], [event] may occur, which would affect [objective] by [consequence].
Because of a single cloud vendor, a regional outage may occur, which would delay the Q3 launch by 4–6 weeks.
