What risk really is — and isn't

Risk is the effect of uncertainty on objectives. Why risk management is a different cognitive mode than assigning work — and what it costs to run it as a compliance checkbox.

01 · Definition

Uncertainty, measured against what you're trying to do

A risk only exists relative to an objective. No objective, no risk — just things that might happen. That is why the first question in any risk conversation is "risk to what?"

Risk

Uncertain. Might happen, would affect an objective.

Issue

Certain. Already happening — manage it, don't score it.

Control gap

A missing or broken control. Often the cause of a risk, not the risk itself.
02 · Two modes

Work assignment vs. risk thinking

Executives are trained to turn ambiguity into tasks. Risk management asks for the opposite: keep the ambiguity visible long enough to decide how much of it you can live with.

How a good risk conversation runs

  1. Name the objective — revenue target, launch date, licence to operate.
  2. Describe the uncertainty — cause, event, consequence in one sentence.
  3. Estimate honestly — likelihood and impact, with the evidence you have.
  4. Decide — treat, accept, or escalate against appetite.
Why it feels uncomfortable
There is no "done" state. A risk is monitored, not closed — which conflicts with how most leadership teams measure progress.
What changes for the board
Boards approve appetite and review movement, not individual scores.

04 · The checkbox cost

What compliance-only risk management costs

The register becomes theatre

Scores are set once for the auditor, never revisited, and treatment plans describe controls nobody operates. The result looks managed and isn't.
