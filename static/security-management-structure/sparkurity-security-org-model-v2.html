Organisational structure, reporting lines and interfaces

The reference structure for an ICT-heavy organisation. Solid lines are reporting lines; dashed lines are interfaces, assurance relationships or on-demand activation. In ICT-light organisations the four pillars remain as capabilities but collapse into fractional and outsourced patterns — see the sourcing matrix. Every element links to its description.

Why the CISO is not under the CIOThe CIO is measured on delivery and uptime; security is a control function on that delivery. DORA makes control-function independence explicit; under NIS2 it is implied by the management body's personal accountability. Minimum viable variant: CISO under CIO administratively, with a guaranteed, unfiltered reporting line to the management body.
Why offensive security is a fourth pillarIf the red team reports through the same manager as the SOC, findings get softened before they reach the CISO and TLPT independence fails. Separate pillar, same CISO — adversarial inside the function, aligned above it. Detail in offensive security.
What stays outside the security organisationManagement body duties (can't be delegated), internal audit (3rd line — audits the CISO too), and the DPO (independence under GDPR Art. 38). The model defines interfaces to these, never ownership of them.

LAYER A Direction Accountability — never outsourced, never automated

Management body oversight

ECSF GAPNEVER DELEGATED

The layer above the CISO. Approves the cybersecurity strategy and risk appetite, oversees implementation of risk-management measures, formally accepts residual risk, and receives security metrics on a defined cadence. Under NIS2 Art. 20 the management body is personally accountable and must undergo cybersecurity training; under DORA Art. 5 it bears ultimate responsibility for ICT risk. These duties cannot be delegated to the CISO, outsourced, or automated.

Concrete duties
  • Approve risk appetite statement and security strategy annually
  • Formally accept residual risks above CISO authority threshold
  • Receive incident and metrics reporting; attend annual training
  • Approve TLPT / major testing programmes and crisis exercises
Interfaces

Audit committee (3rd-line assurance) · CISO (unfiltered reporting line) · supervisory authority (accountability)

Related

Risk management · Crisis & resilience · Why this is an ECSF gap

Security leadership (CISO)

01NEVER ABSENT

Owns strategy, budget, the security organisation and the interface to the management body. Independent of the CIO/CTO delivery line — explicitly required as a control function under DORA, and structurally necessary wherever security must be able to say no to delivery. May be fractional (vCISO) in ICT-light organisations, but never absent and never anonymous: a named person carries the role.

Concrete duties
  • Security strategy, budget and organisational design (this model)
  • Risk acceptance within delegated threshold; escalation above it
  • Owns AI governance to prevent orphaning between legal, IT and data
  • Approves red team rules of engagement and TLPT scope
Interfaces

Management body · CIO/CTO (peer) · DPO · internal audit · supervisory authority / national CSIRT

Related

Reporting-line design · Crisis management

LAYER B · 2nd line Governance Sets rules, measures risk, verifies

Risk management

10

Owns the risk register, assessment methodology and cadence, treatment tracking and reporting. Second line: assesses risk but never accepts it — acceptance sits with risk owners, the CISO, or the management body depending on threshold. This assessor ≠ acceptor split is the incompatibility rule that makes fractional staffing safe.

Interfaces

Business risk owners · third-party security (vendor risk feeds) · vulnerability management (technical risk feeds) · internal audit

Related

AI governance · Crisis & resilience

Policy, compliance & audit coordination

0306

Policy lifecycle, regulatory mapping (NIS2, DORA, GDPR, AI Act, sector regimes), evidence management and the interface to internal and external audit. The audit function itself stays third-line and independent — this capability coordinates with it, never performs it. Control operator ≠ control auditor.

Interfaces

Internal audit · external auditors and certification bodies · legal · data protection

Related

Risk management · Human risk

Third-party & supply chain security

1003ECSF THIN

Vendor security assessment, contractual security requirements, concentration-risk monitoring, exit strategies and the register of ICT third-party arrangements. Explicit in NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d); the dominant function under DORA (register of information, critical ICT third-party providers). For ICT-light organisations this is often the single most important capability — their posture is their vendor management.

Interfaces

Procurement (embedded in sourcing gates) · legal (contracts) · risk management · business owners of vendor relationships

Related

Human risk management

07

Awareness programmes, role-based training (including mandatory management-body training under NIS2), phishing simulation, and insider-risk signals in cooperation with HR. By 2026 delivery is substantially AI-personalised — content per role, language and prior behaviour — with the Educator profile curating rather than lecturing.

Interfaces

HR (onboarding, insider risk, sanctions) · communications · all business units

Related

Management body training duty

AI governance

031009ECSF GAP

AI usage policy and shadow-AI handling, the AI system inventory and risk classification required by the EU AI Act, security requirements for AI systems the organisation builds or buys, and oversight rules for agentic automation inside the security function itself (who is accountable for what an agent does). Owned by the CISO in this model, precisely because no ECSF profile owns it and it otherwise orphans between legal, IT and data teams.

Interfaces

Legal (AI Act obligations) · DPO (data in AI systems) · secure SDLC (AI in the pipeline) · AI security testing

Related

Why this is an ECSF gap · Non-human identity

Data protection interface

03

A defined RACI between security and the DPO: breach assessment and notification (72h GDPR vs 24h/72h NIS2 tracks run in parallel), DPIA security input, records of processing alignment. The DPO itself stays independent under GDPR Art. 38 — the model defines the interface, never absorbs the role.

Interfaces

DPO · legal · incident response (breach track)

LAYER C · 1st line Defensive operations Detect, respond, recover — increasingly AI-augmented

Security monitoring / SOC

0208

Detection engineering, alert triage, threat hunting, telemetry pipeline ownership. Tier-1 triage and enrichment are substantially agentic by 2026; the standard therefore requires a named human owner for detection coverage and for every automated action class — the accountability sits above the automation, not inside it. Receives detection improvements from purple exercises.

Interfaces

IT operations (telemetry sources) · MSSP/MDR where outsourced · threat intelligence · incident response (escalation)

Threat intelligence

04

Collects and contextualises threat data for both blue and red: detection priorities for the SOC, realistic scenarios for the red team. TLPT is threat-led by definition, and under DORA the TI provider for TLPT must be external — one reason this stays a named capability even when fully consumed as a service.

Interfaces

National CSIRT and sector ISACs · TI vendors · vulnerability management (exploitation context)

Vulnerability management

1208ECSF SPLIT

Continuous scanning, prioritisation (exploitability × exposure × asset criticality — SSVC-style decision trees), patch coordination with IT, and SLA tracking. Deliberately split from the red team: this is always-on and operational; red teaming is periodic and adversarial. Receives validated findings from red team operations and attack surface validation, and feeds technical risk into the register.

Interfaces

IT operations (patching) · AppSec (code-level findings) · third-party security (vendor vulnerabilities)

Incident response

02VIRTUAL TEAM

The canonical virtual-team capability: a small permanent core (incident manager, technical lead) with pre-nominated, pre-trained members from IT, legal, communications and HR who activate on declaration. Pre-assigned owners for the NIS2 24-hour early warning and 72-hour notification, and the parallel GDPR 72-hour breach track. Escalates to crisis management when impact crosses defined thresholds.

Interfaces

All virtual-team home functions · national CSIRT (notifications) · forensics · DPO (breach track)

Digital forensics

11

Evidence preservation, artefact analysis, chain of custody. Retainer-based for almost every organisation — the capability that must exist as a contract and a runbook before the incident, because procurement during an incident fails. Internal minimum: first-responder evidence-preservation skills inside the IR core.

Interfaces

Law enforcement · legal (privilege, admissibility) · insurers

Crisis management & resilience

0110

The escalation layer above IR: business continuity, disaster-recovery testing, backup verification, crisis communications, and regulator/customer notification workflows. Chaired at executive level — the CISO advises, the business decides. DORA's digital operational resilience testing programme lives here, alongside the annual crisis exercise the management body approves.

Interfaces

Executive team · communications · facilities · key customers and suppliers

LAYER D · 1st line Engineering Build security in, verify it continuously

Security architecture

05

Design-time: reference architectures, security patterns, technology selection, threat modelling of new systems. Distinct from gate-time review in the SDLC — one shapes what gets built, the other checks what was built. In small organisations one person wears both hats; the model still names both so the design activity doesn't silently disappear.

Interfaces

Enterprise/solution architects under CIO · product management · AI governance (architecture of AI systems)

Secure SDLC / AppSec

0805

Gate-time: pipeline guardrails, policy-as-code, dependency and secrets scanning, security review of changes. AI-generated code multiplies change volume, so the capability shifts from human line-review toward automated gates with AI-assisted review and human sampling — the standard defines the sampling rate and the classes of change that always require human sign-off.

Interfaces

Engineering teams (embedded champions) · vulnerability management · red team (findings loop)

Identity & access governance

08ECSF THIN

Joiner-mover-leaver, privileged access, access recertification — and increasingly non-human identity: service accounts, workload identities and AI-agent credentials, which now grow faster than human identities. Every agent identity gets an owner, a scope and an expiry; agentic AI without identity governance is the new shadow IT.

Interfaces

HR (JML triggers) · IT operations (directory, PAM) · AI governance (agent authority rules)

Related

Why non-human identity is an ECSF gap

Offensive testing (red team)

12INDEPENDENT OF BLUE

Periodic adversarial simulation against people, processes and technology, threat-led scenarios, TLPT under DORA. Organisationally separate from the SOC — same CISO, different pillar — so findings reach leadership unsoftened. Full operating model, team composition and maturity ladder in the offensive security section.

Interfaces

Vulnerability management (findings intake) · threat intelligence (scenarios) · SOC (purple exercises, deconfliction)

AI security testing & research

0912

Adversarial testing of AI systems: prompt injection, jailbreak resistance, data poisoning, model extraction, agent-authority abuse — OWASP LLM Top 10 territory. Sits inside offensive security for AI-heavy organisations; consumed as specialist service otherwise. The Researcher profile contributes methods; the Penetration Tester contributes operations.

Interfaces

AI governance (test obligations per risk class) · product engineering · external AI security researchers

Offensive security inside the organisation

Not a procurement line item — an organisational function. For ICT-heavy organisations the red team is internal, continuous and structurally independent of blue; for ICT-light organisations the same capability is delivered through PTaaS and periodic external engagements, but the operating rules below still apply to whoever performs the work.

Team composition

Offensive security lead
Owns scoping, rules of engagement, safety and the findings pipeline. Reports directly to the CISO — never through the SOC manager. Translates findings into business risk language for the management body.
1201
Adversary emulation operators
Execute threat-led scenarios end to end: initial access, lateral movement, objective actions. Scenario realism comes from threat intelligence, not from the operators' favourite techniques.
12
AI red teamer
Adversarial testing of the organisation's AI systems and AI-enabled attack simulation against the organisation. Carries AI security testing; methods from the Researcher profile, operations from the Penetration Tester profile.
0912
Tooling & infrastructure engineer
Maintains attack infrastructure, implants and automation safely — segregated from production, with evidence handling that survives audit. Often fractional with AppSec in mid-size teams (allowed: builder of red tooling; not allowed: approver of the changes they test).
1208
Purple exercise facilitation
Not a standing team — a scheduled joint activity. Red replays techniques with blue watching; every undetected step becomes a detection-engineering backlog item for the SOC. This feedback loop is the main economic argument for having red in-house at all.
1202
Attack surface validation
Continuous, largely automated: external attack surface discovery, breach-and-attack simulation, control validation (CTEM-style). The always-on complement to periodic campaigns; validated exposures route into vulnerability management.
12AI-AUGMENTED

Operating rules

  • Reporting line: offensive security lead → CISO directly. Findings are never routed through, or softened by, the function being tested.
  • Scoping authority: rules of engagement approved by the CISO and the affected system owner; management body approves the annual programme and any TLPT.
  • Deconfliction: a sealed-envelope protocol with the SOC lead — exercises are detectable as exercises within minutes when safety requires, without pre-warning the floor.
  • Findings pipeline: every validated finding enters vulnerability management with an owner and SLA; every undetected technique enters the SOC detection backlog. No report-and-forget.
  • TLPT under DORA (Art. 26–27): the threat-intelligence provider must be external; internal testers may be used only under strict independence and resourcing conditions — in practice, alternate internal and external cycles.
  • People and process in scope: social engineering and physical vectors require explicit, per-campaign approval and HR/legal sign-off — never blanket authorisation.

Maturity ladder

  1. Annual external pentest — compliance-driven, point-in-time. The ICT-light baseline.
  2. Continuous validation — PTaaS, attack surface management, bug bounty. Findings flow into vuln management continuously.
  3. Internal red + purple loop — threat-led campaigns, detection engineering feedback, social/physical vectors. The ICT-heavy target.
  4. Adversarial exposure management — CTEM operating rhythm, AI-augmented emulation, AI systems in scope, TLPT-ready.

The twelve ECSF role profiles

Click a profile for its mission and the capabilities it carries in this model. Chip colour indicates the layer where the profile does most of its work.

01Chief Information Security Officer

Manages the organisation's cybersecurity strategy and its implementation; the accountability interface to executive management.

Carries

Security leadership · Crisis & resilience (chair-advisor) · owns AI governance

02Cyber Incident Responder

Monitors, assesses and responds to incidents; restores systems and processes to operational state. The ECSF folds monitoring/detection work into this profile.

Carries

SOC & monitoring · Incident response · blue side of purple exercises

03Cyber Legal, Policy & Compliance Officer

Manages compliance with cybersecurity-related legal and regulatory frameworks and internal policy.

Carries

Policy & compliance · Data protection interface · shares third-party security and AI governance

04Cyber Threat Intelligence Specialist

Collects, processes and analyses threat data into actionable intelligence.

Carries

Threat intelligence — feeding both blue and red

05Cybersecurity Architect

Plans and designs security solutions and secure system architectures.

Carries

Security architecture · shares secure SDLC

06Cybersecurity Auditor

Performs independent cybersecurity audits against standards and regulation. Third line — independence is the point.

Carries

Audit side of policy, compliance & audit coordination; audits everything else in this model, including the CISO

07Cybersecurity Educator

Improves cybersecurity knowledge, skills and awareness across the organisation.

Carries

Human risk management, including the management body training duty

08Cybersecurity Implementer

Develops, deploys and operates cybersecurity solutions and controls.

Carries

Secure SDLC / AppSec · Identity & access · tooling in SOC and vuln management

09Cybersecurity Researcher

Researches the cybersecurity domain; converts findings into methods, prototypes and innovation. Optional for ICT-light; near-mandatory for AI-heavy product organisations.

Carries

Methods for AI security testing · contributes to AI governance

10Cybersecurity Risk Manager

Manages the risk assessment and treatment process; maintains the organisation's risk picture.

Carries

Risk management · shares third-party security and resilience

11Digital Forensics Investigator

Collects, preserves and analyses digital evidence with chain of custody.

Carries

Digital forensics — retainer-based in most organisations

12Penetration Tester

Assesses control effectiveness by identifying and exploiting vulnerabilities.

Carries

Red team · operations side of AI security testing · shares vulnerability management · attack surface validation

Where the ECSF runs out

Four capabilities the model requires that the twelve profiles do not cleanly own. These are the extension points the Sparkurity method adds on top of the framework.

Management body oversight

The ECSF stops at the CISO. NIS2 Art. 20 makes the management body personally accountable for approving and overseeing risk measures — a defined layer above every profile, with duties that cannot be delegated, outsourced, or automated.

MODEL EXTENSION → LAYER A, HARD RULE

AI governance

No profile owns AI usage policy, shadow-AI handling, AI system inventory and risk classification, or security of AI systems themselves. Fragments sit in profiles 03, 09 and 10 — the model assigns ownership explicitly to the CISO to keep it from being orphaned.

COMPOSITE OF 03 + 09 + 10, OWNED BY 01

Third-party & supply chain security

Explicit in NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d) and dominant under DORA, yet the ECSF spreads it across the Risk Manager and Compliance Officer. For ICT-light organisations this is often the single most important capability — their posture is their vendor management.

COMPOSITE OF 10 + 03, FIRST-CLASS CAPABILITY

Non-human identity governance

Service accounts, workload identities and AI-agent credentials are growing faster than human identities. The Implementer profile touches IAM operationally, but nobody owns the governance of machine identity at the rate agentic AI is creating it.
